# The three joints, and what they stand for

The hall's own notes say the model "is not a buildable mortise-and-tenon model": its pieces are
drawn overlapping where they join. The three joints below are cut into copies so a close-up can
show the joint types Liang Sicheng's report describes for a hall of this kind. They are
illustrations of the types, sized plausibly, not measured reconstructions.

**Column-top tenon, 馒头榫.** A square tenon on the top of a column enters a mortise in the
underside of the big block (栌斗) that starts the bracket set. Tool sizes: 16 cm square, 12 cm
deep, cut through the block's foot into its seat. Explode: the block lifts 50 cm.

**Cross-lap, 十字卯口.** Inside the big block two arms cross at one level: the 华拱 running out
from the wall and the 泥道拱 running along it. Each loses half its depth at the crossing, the
upper arm from below and the lower from above, so the two nest flush. Tool sizes: the notch is
the overlap of the two arms in plan, half the arm depth. Explode: the notched-from-below arm
lifts 35 cm, the other stays.

**Dovetail, 燕尾榫.** The tie beam between columns (阑额) ends in a dovetail that drops from
above into a matching slot in the column, so it cannot pull out. Tool sizes: the last 30 cm of
the beam flares 6 cm each side; the column's slot is the same shape, open at the top. Explode:
the beam lifts its own depth plus 25 cm.

The pull-apart orders pieces by the height of their bottom. The sources give the assembly of a
bracket set tier by tier from the big block upward, which is the same order for a bracket set
alone; for a whole bay the tie beams go in before the blocks, which the bearing order from
model-anatomy also says.
