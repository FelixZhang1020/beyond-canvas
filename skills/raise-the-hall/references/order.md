# Why the carrying order is the construction order

A piece cannot be placed before what carries it exists, so the bearing graph model-anatomy
measures (what each piece rests on, seats and near-misses, with pieces merely drawn through
something above them not counted) gives a construction order by itself: a piece's stage is one
more than the highest stage of its carriers, ground being stage zero. On the Foguang hall v25
that is 36 stages, columns first and the roof covering last, which `stages.py` merges into at
most 24 named scenes so the video reads at a glance.

What the sources say about a hall of this kind: the columns and the tie beams between them go
up first; the big blocks sit on the column tops; the bracket sets rise tier by tier, arms and
blocks alternating; the beams and purlins follow; then the rafters, the boarding, the clay bed
and the tiles. The measured order agrees with this in every band.

Where the model's order departs from a real build: all 36 columns arrive in one scene, where a
crew raised them one by one; the between-column arms the bearing tool finds floating (a known
gap in the model) arrive with the columns; and the whole roof covering arrives as the last
scene, where tiling took weeks. These are choices for a ten-second video, not claims.
