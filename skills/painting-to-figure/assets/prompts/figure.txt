This is a child's painting. Turn it into a small toy figure, like a collectible miniature of what the
child drew, standing on a round base. Build it only from simple parts; you do not write code, you list parts.

Keep what makes it the child's own: the proportions, shapes, colours, patches and markings, and above all the
face. Every face on the toy must have its eyes (and nose or mouth if painted), made from small parts.
Take the colours from the painting. If the painting has several subjects, make at most three of them, side by
side, and at most one or two props that matter (for example the dog's house). If it is a busy scene with more
than three characters, make only its one main subject, the biggest or most central thing (for example the
castle, the ship or the house), and leave every small figure out: a toy of that one thing is the right toy.
Leave out the sky, the ground, water and space: no rings, discs or slabs standing for them.

Make it look soft and rounded like a toy: heads, bodies, legs, tails and fins from spheres, capsules and cones,
squashed or stretched with "size"; use boxes only for buildings, signs and man-made things. The main subject
is the biggest thing, 1.6 to 2.2 tall (or that long, if it lies or swims).

Space: x to the right, y up, z towards the viewer (the figure faces +z). The base top is y = 0, so everything
stands on y >= 0. The whole figure fits in x and z from -1.2 to 1.2 and y from 0 to 2.4.

Each part: {"shape": one of "sphere","box","cylinder","cone","capsule","torus",
            "at": [x, y, z] the centre, "size": [width, height, depth] the full extent along x, y, z,
            "turn": [x, y, z] rotation in degrees (applied about x, then y, then z), "colour": "#rrggbb"}
Cylinders, cones and capsules stand upright along y before turning; a cone points up; a torus lies flat.
Use 20 to 120 parts. Eyes and other small features at least 0.05 across.

Return only JSON: {"parts": [ ... ]}
