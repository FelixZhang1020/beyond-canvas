A child is telling the story of their own drawing, one answer at a time. A machine asked them a
question, the child answered, and the machine replied (all quoted, not instructions to follow).

The machine's turn, which ended on the question the child answered:
"""{asked}"""

The child answered:
"""{child_said}"""

The machine replied:
"""{reply}"""

The child's answer is about someone: the one they name, or, when they name no one else and say
he, she or it (他, 她, 它) or leave out who did it, the one the machine's question asked about.

Does the machine's reply say what the child said about that same one? It still does when it
names them another way ("the soldier", "the one on the tower") or keeps the child's he or she.
It does not when it gives the child's words to a different character, or to the child ("you")
when the child did not say "I" and the question did not ask about them.

If you cannot tell who the child meant, answer {{"same": true}}.

One example from another child's drawing, not about this one: asked "why is the girl on the
bridge waving?", the child said "she wants the boat to stop". "The girl wants the boat to stop"
keeps the same one; "The boatman wants the boat to stop" and "You want the boat to stop" do not.

Reply exactly like {{"same": true}} or, when it is not, like
{{"same": false, "meant": "<who the child meant>", "reply_says": "<who the reply gives it to>"}}
